> *"Clad in glass, stone or stainless steel, the exterior of a building represents its public face. It's more than aesthetics. Years of building regulations, egos, cultural influences, safety concerns and economics have shaped and altered the form of skyscraper architecture. The same thing goes for the LA behemoth we're building from the ground up. Now, Building a Skyscraper, The Exterior, on Modern Marvels. By the spring of 2003, the builders of the new California Department of Transportation headquarters have spent 10 months digging, redesigning, shooting, lifting, bolting, and welding on what will be a 750,000 square foot office building in downtown Los Angeles. The internal steel skeleton has topped out, and now it's time to put a face on the structure, give it an identity. Some believe what a structure looks like is the most important aspect of any large modern edifice. The skyscraper is a symbol of economic power, willpower, technological innovation, all bound up in one highly visible structure that defines the appearance and character of our cities. have always used diverse materials to give classic buildings character. Terracotta on the Woolworth Building, stainless steel on the Chrysler Building, or glass on Leverhaus. Tom Main of Morphosis Architects chose to set the Caltrans Building apart by cladding it in perforated aluminum panels."*
