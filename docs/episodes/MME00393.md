> How do you build creature comforts into a tower of steel and glass? From freight hoists to high-speed pressurized elevators. From ice to nice. In Los Angeles, we're constructing a mega building from the ground up. And we'll use it to explore the development of the systems that make the high life comfortable while meeting the people who design them. them. Now, Building a Skyscraper, The Human Environment, on Modern Marvels. For the past year, eight months, and 20 days, the builders of the new California Department of Transportation Headquarters in downtown Los Angeles have been busy pouring concrete, erecting steel, and installing walls and windows. The massive 750,000-square-foot office building looks almost completed from the outside, but the structure is still pretty naked on the inside. It'll take another seven months to finish installing the heating, ventilation, and air conditioning system and elevators. These are the things that make working in a large office building possible, even bearable. Beyond the introduction of steel cage construction, no invention helped buildings, including our own, climb higher than the elevator. If we did not have passenger elevators, we don't have the modern multi-story office building because people won't walk more than two or three floors off the street to easily reach an office. The Caltrans building will have 19 elevators to shuttle its 2,000 employees to upper floors. Kone Incorporated will soon begin installing them. But in the meantime, Clark Construction is running a temporary man lift to move workers and materials.
