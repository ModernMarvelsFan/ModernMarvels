### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, Titanic Tech on Modern Marvels."*

> The Titanic. Watertight compartments in a steel-plated hull render it all but impenetrable. Nearly every technological breakthrough of the previous 50 years is on board, providing comfort and safety. If it was built to be unsinkable, then why did it sink? Now, Titanic Tech on Modern Marvels. The immensity of its floating hulk was too great to be contained in a single glance. Surely it weighed hundreds of thousands of tons. Much of its bulk rested beneath the surface of the calm seas. It must have glistened even on a moonless April night on its maiden voyage. Most marvelous of all, it was created entirely out of water, the iceberg that sank the Titanic. Perhaps the story of the Titanic begins here, in the frigid North Atlantic, where icebergs are calved, broken off from glaciers that drop into the ocean off Greenland's coast. It was here that the battle between technology and nature played out on that mid-April evening in 1912. And it was here that technology lost. How do you take the largest ship in the world on its maiden voyage, full of the wealthiest, most influential people of two continents, you know, that's called unsinkable, and crash it in the middle of the Atlantic before it ever completes its maiden voyage? between 10 and 15 thousand icebergs form every year off greenland some nearly three million tons and containing ice as old as 110 thousand years and they continue to present a shipping hazard they're deteriorating by the time they make it to the grand banks and eventually south out into the shipping lanes typically they're very dramatic in shape you know they've taken on irregular shapes and they're beautiful, but they're also very dangerous.
