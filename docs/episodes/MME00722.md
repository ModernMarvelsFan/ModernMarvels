### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> It can be enjoyed as a solid or as a liquid. It can be served hot, cold, room temperature, or frozen. It's an essential part in religious rites, holiday traditions, even romantic gestures. And when placed into a box, it can serve as a metaphor for the uncertainty of life itself. But today, unlike life itself, I know exactly what I'm going to get. Chocolate. Even though the United States has less than 5% to the world's population, it consumes close to 20% of the world's chocolate. The United States eats almost 100 pounds of chocolate every second of every day. And chocolate generates $100 billion in worldwide revenues per year. Whether it comes in a truffle, a cup of hot cocoa, or a scoop of ice cream, nothing compares to the decadent indulgence of chocolate. And today, we're doing a deep dive on chocolate candy. Where does the word chocolate come from? What are some of the bizarre foods that people coat in chocolate? And how did Milton Hershey revolutionize the way we enjoy this beloved sweet treat? How does this chocolate economy work? How does a tropical fruit grown around the equator become one of the world's most beloved sweets? And how many tiny cocoa beans does it take to keep up with this gigantic demand? There's only one way to find out. This is Modern Marvel's Chocolate. Today, I'm in the city of brotherly love, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, the US's first home to Independence Hall and the Liberty Bell. For a while, Philadelphia was one of the most active ports in the U.S. Because of that, most of the cacao beans entering the United States during the 19th century were processed right here. About 100 years before that, in 1739, Benjamin Franklin was selling chocolate out of his print shop
