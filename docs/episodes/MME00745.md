### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"You'll never boot up your tech treasures the same way again on Modern Marvels."*

> Today, they're the most commonly used devices on the planet. Technology is moving very quickly. A total revolution in how we talk, watch, and listen. I can just throw away this tape because it's all digital. But today's high-tech wonders weren't always so wonderful. From turntables to typewriters, cathode rays to cassettes, These are the gadgets that were once state-of-the-art. You'll never boot up your tech treasures the same way again on Modern Marvels. Not that long ago, these relics were parts that lived inside our favorite gadgets. the ones we absolutely couldn't live without, the ones we reluctantly retired to attics and garages. These seemingly primitive products were once our connection to the world and to each other. And no matter what new innovations we embrace, we'll always recall the retro originals with nostalgic fondness. Remember that cumbersome cathode ray tube TV set and its annoying antenna? For decades, this clunky icon was your ticket to a world of news and entertainment. We didn't pay for anything. Advertising paid for everything. You didn't have a cable box. You didn't have wires connecting it. You didn't have a satellite dish. To get that free TV signal, you either had an aerial roof antenna or a pair of rabbit ears sitting atop the set. The CRT TV owes its size and heft to the large tube and the lead-laden glass screen that protects the viewer from the harmful x-rays generated inside. In a CRT, the cathode is a heated filament. It sits in a vacuum inside a glass tube. The ray is a stream of electrons that pours off the cathode, becomes focused into a beam, and strikes the phosphor-coated back of the screen, making it glow.
