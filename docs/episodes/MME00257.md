### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Modern Marvels will return in a moment."*

> The warplane was born at the start of World War I, when fragile British and German spotter aircraft first ventured over the trenches of the Western Front. They were made of wood, wire, and canvas. By the end of the war in 1918, air battles involving bombers and fighters had become an established part of modern warfare. By the 1930s, very little had changed. The aircraft were still biplanes. They were still made of wood covered with fabric, and they were slow. Then, in 1931, British seaplane reached the astonishing speed of 407 miles per hour, more than twice as fast as the biplanes then in service with most air forces. The S-6B was a streamlined metal monoplane far ahead of its time. It was the latest in a series of racing planes created by the Supermarine Aircraft Company. Its chief designer, Reginald Mitchell, was a brilliant engineer who understood that the day of the traditional biplane was over. In 1930, the British government officially requested that its aircraft industry submit designs for a new fighter. Mitchell produced an aircraft which stuck closely to the government's request, but was little better than the biplanes it was meant to replace. Mitchell went back to the drawing board. Then, in 1933, a new sense of urgency developed. The Nazi party was voted into power in Germany. Its leader, Adolf Hitler, began rearming his country with modern all-metal bombers and fighters.
