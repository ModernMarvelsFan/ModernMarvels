### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, Cold Cuts on Modern Marvels."*

> Cold and cut with precision. They're the meat in our sandwiches and slices of American pop culture. They're the source of long-held secrets and the inspiration from mile-high masterpieces. Delectable and dodgy. This is pork tongue and pork snout that has been cooked. Pureed, pumped or poured. Now, Cold Cuts on Modern Marvels. Cold cuts. They're cold and they're cut. Whether at the sandwich shop or deli counter, a picnic or sporting event, cold cuts are the ultimate inconvenience food. And they've been that way since they began arriving with European immigrants in the 1800s. They've also become extremely profitable. Every year, Americans consume over $2 billion worth. And although dozens of different cold cuts fill our sandwiches, a few stand out. Some have even become a part of the American lexicon. Like this one. Farmland Foods in Wichita, Kansas is full of baloney. Every year, this plant squeezes out 60 million pounds of it. Enough to make 960 million sandwiches. But what exactly have Americans been putting in their lunchboxes for all these years? The answer rests in these 2,000-pound combo babies. I'm surrounded right here by the meat ingredients that go into a bologna. Right here, we have what they call 50 trim. That meat will be 50% lean, 50% fat, and will go into this particular combo. Over here, we have the pork trimmings. They'll take the trimmings from the loin, from the ham, and this particular meat will be 80% lean, 20% fat. The last thing we have, and the more predominant in bologna, is mechanically separated chicken. Okay, we know you're wondering. Mechanically separating chicken is the process of removing the remaining meat from the bones of an already butchered chicken.
