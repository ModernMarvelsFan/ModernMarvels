### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> If digging ditches is your game, these machines are the biggest and baddest shovels in the pit. From the legendary steam shovel, to super shovels with teeth, and monster shovels with bigger teeth. The latest scoops are stronger, faster, and even smarter than ever. Now, shovels dig it on Modern Marbles. Consider the shovel. It may seem uncomplicated, just a blade on a stick. Yet it's actually a precision-designed alloy steel tool that's built to last. They're stamped out by the millions, in dozens of shapes and styles for every possible task. Yet the venerable hand shovel that helped create civilization is limited by the strength of its user. But when human strength is taken out of the equation, the shovel can get bigger, much bigger. Meet the P&H Mining 4100A Electric Mining Shovel. U.S. Borax operates two of them. And if moving rock and dirt in a hurry is the game plan, nothing else even comes close. From the ground to the tip of its massive boom, this shovel stands seven stories tall. Its dipper weighs 85 tons. We run our 4,100s 24 hours a day, seven days a week, 354 days a year. We typically get 20 hours of operating time out of a shovel each day. It's typically scooping up a little over 80 tons. You can put three dippers in a haul truck in just a little over a minute. In 24 hours' time, these shovels will load 140,000 tons apiece. This is California's largest surface mine, operated by U.S. Borax. Borax is a major ingredient in hundreds of industrial and household products. Beginning in 1919, the mine was underground.
