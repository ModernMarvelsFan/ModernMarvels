### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> Civilians under attack. The images are surreal and unsettling. In the 1950s, the threat of a Soviet nuclear attack spurred an arms race, and Americans learned to duck and cover. Today, there are new enemies wielding new weapons. Duck and cover has been replaced by duct tape and plastic sheeting. And there's a new technological arms race underway, as science tries to stay one step ahead of the terrorist threat. Find out what technology can do to keep you and your family safe. June 22, 2001. A senior level war game codenamed Dark Winter gets underway at Andrews Air Force Base in Washington, DC. Wargaming is a time-honored practice in the US military to test readiness and strategy. But this scenario is different. This time, the fictional enemy is a terrorist group. The target is the American public. And the weapon is a biological agent, smallpox. The fictional scenario begins with terrorists unleashing the deadly virus in shopping malls in three states. The purpose of the dark winter exercise was to see how ready the country was to deal with this new type of threat. How ready were we? We shall see. The threat of large-scale attacks on American civilians is not new. Long before the rise of international terrorist groups, Americans had plenty to fear from foreign armies.
