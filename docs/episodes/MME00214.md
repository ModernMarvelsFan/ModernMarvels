### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Million Dollar Tech will return on Modern Marvels."*

<details>
<summary><b>Click to view full opening narration (~2 minutes)</b></summary>

> *"These high-end holdings are the province of the super-rich. Priceless possessions that allow wealthy men to indulge their passions, harness their obsessions, and flex their mechanical muscles. Exclusivity, technological complexity, manly competition, and just plain fun have driven many kingly consumers to covet, create, and purchase these outrageous items. They come with million dollar tech specs that aren't necessarily measured by cost, but by a state of mind. Male aspirations to own the biggest, the baddest, the best. I think over the centuries, men of wealth have surrounded themselves with all types of toys just to differentiate themselves from the masses. And basically, you know, it just boils down to I do it because I can. and they want to show that power. But there's a lot of hard work and there's a lot of thought and there's a lot of ingenuity. There's a lot of art and craftsmanship that go into making these things. Many of these power playthings have involved transportation, allowing moguls to travel fast, in luxury, and with increased privacy. At the turn of the century, many wealthy American industrialists, such as John D. Rockefeller, Andrew Carnegie, Henry Ford, and Jay Gould were renowned for their opulent personal railway cars, a forerunner of today's private corporate jet. Gould's rail car, the St. Louis, had ornate lighting fixtures, vaulted ceilings, and an elaborate carved wood decor. Today, there are about 80 private railway cars operating in the U.S. John Kirkwood owns Eight, which he rents to well-heeled customers. For personal travel, he hooks up a car to the back of Amtrak and takes off. When you're on a train, there's a romance and a feel and just a texture of it that's just delightful. From the smells to the sounds to the way it rides, it just gets in your blood."*

</details>
