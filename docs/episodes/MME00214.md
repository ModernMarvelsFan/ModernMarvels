Exclusivity, technological complexity, manly competition, and just plain fun have driven many kingly consumers to covet, create, and purchase these outrageous items. They come with million dollar tech specs that aren't necessarily measured by cost, but by a state of mind. Male aspirations to own the biggest, the baddest, the best. Many of these power playthings have involved transportation, allowing moguls to travel fast, in luxury, and with increased privacy. At the turn of the century, many wealthy American industrialists, such as John D. Rockefeller, Andrew Carnegie, Henry Ford, and Jay Gould were renowned for their opulent personal railway cars, a forerunner of today's private corporate jet. Gould's rail car, the St. Louis, had ornate lighting fixtures, vaulted ceilings, and an elaborate carved wood decor. Today, there are about 80 private railway cars operating in the U.S. John Kirkwood owns Eight, which he rents to well-heeled customers. For personal travel, he hooks up a car to the back of Amtrak and takes off. When you're on a train, there's a romance and a feel and just a texture of it that's just delightful. From the smells to the sounds to the way it rides, it just gets in your blood. Kirkwood recently purchased and renovated the Bella Vista, a 1955 Union Pacific rail car at a cost of more than $1 million.
