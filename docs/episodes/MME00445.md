### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, Sub-Zero on Modern Marvels."*

> Engines freeze. Frostbite consumes limbs. Death in the extreme cove is just a mistake away. Yet, a small city thrives atop 9,000 feet of ice. A probe hurdles through the blistering cold of deep space. A ship opens a lifeline in the frozen sea. Now, Sub-Zero on Modern Marvels. If you're able to withstand its icy grip, the cold can be a gateway to both material and spiritual enrichment. From the billions of barrels of oil pouring out of the north shore of Alaska to communities accustomed to minus 40 degrees Fahrenheit. It's just living in a place that automatically challenges you and is frankly never very boring. To science. When you dig down into the ice, you'll be looking at ice that was formed a thousand years ago, or a hundred thousand years ago, or five hundred thousand years ago. To recreation. Cold temperatures are exhilarating. You're pushed to the edge. You've got to be focused all the time. You make a mistake, you're going to pay. Shelter in these cold and often barren locales has always been a challenge. Few places on Earth offer as many obstacles to building and sustaining a structure as the South Pole. The geographic South Pole, the bottom of the world, sits atop more than 9,000 feet of snow and ice. Temperatures during the austral winter can dip to minus 117 degrees Fahrenheit. And the ice beneath your feet is literally moving, drifting 33 feet a year. So why build anything at the coldest, most remote locale on Earth? Turns out that because it's so dry and it's 10,000 feet elevation, South Pole Station is one of the world's best places for certain kinds of astrophysics.
