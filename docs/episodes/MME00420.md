On October 4, 1992, an El Al Boeing 747 freighter took off from Schiphol Airport near Amsterdam. It was 5.20 p.m. The three-man crew and one passenger were headed to Tel Aviv to deliver a routine cargo shipment. But the flight turned out to be anything but routine. Shortly after takeoff, the co-pilot sent a distress call to the control tower. Mayday, mayday, we have an emergency. LL-1862, do you wish to return to Schiphol? Roger, we have fire on engine number 3. Roger, 1862. Schiphol, LL-1862, we have an emergency. Number 3 and number 4 engine in operative. Request to 7 for landing. Only seven miles into the flight, the pilot turned around and attempted to return to the airport. His closest runway was the crosswind runway, which was, I believe he had almost 30 knots of wind directly crosswind. And to try to line up the airplane to the runway with two engines gone on one side, limited controllability, but almost impossible. The jet was making its final approach to the runway when the pilot suddenly lost control of the giant 747. The plane plummeted at full speed toward a residential section of Belmameer near Amsterdam.
