### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, the Coliseum on Modern Marvels."*

> It's the world's first stadium, a 2,000-year-old masterpiece that contemporary designers still regard with awe. It may have held as many as 80,000 people and shook for centuries with a savage entertainment that continues to fascinate and appall. A thousand gladiators are said to have died here in a single day. Now, the Coliseum on Modern Marvels. Nothing evokes the greatness and brutality of ancient Rome better than the fallen giant known as the Colosseum. This was once the heartbeat of an empire where huge crowds reveled in gladiatorial combat that reflected and reinforced a larger Roman drama of warfare and conquest, of life and death. In terms of size and scale, this 2,000-year-old stadium with its four levels and 80 entrances would be impressive if it were built today. With a capacity that some experts put as high as 80,000, The Colosseum is still one of the largest stadiums in the world, a structural wonder that was built nearly 2,000 years ahead of its time. This kind of theaters and amphitheaters were realized all over the world during the Roman dominion, but no one ever reached this dimension. From the structural point of view, that was a very relevant accomplishment. I'm sure it was very impressive 2,000 years ago as it is today, perhaps even more so, because now we are used to it through our information channels. But there was no television 2,000 years ago, so people, when they came to Rome, that is the first time they actually saw the whole building. So I think it must have been very great. There's a wonderful line that's attributed to the venerable Bede, living around 700, who said that as long as the Colosseum stands, Rome will stand, and when the Colosseum falls, Rome will fall, and when Rome falls, the world falls. You can't do any better than that as far as a building's concerned.
