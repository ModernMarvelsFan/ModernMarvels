### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> Roll up your sleeves. Get down. Get dirty. As these mechanical monsters drill, plow, and haul mountains and soil. From hundreds of feet below the Earth, to the deepest depths of the oceans, to neighboring planets within our solar system. Now, more Earthmovers on Modern Marvelous. They make the ground tremble. They change the landscape. They move the earth. They may seem like the world's biggest toys, but this is no game. These are multi-million dollar workhorses that support billion dollar industries. that support billion-dollar industries, scooping out Earth's riches, relocating Earth's muck, crushing big rocks into little rocks, sometimes saving the environment, sometimes creating environmental havoc. The earthmoving industry is constantly pushing the boundaries of size and power. Today's Earth movers dig deeper, tunnel farther, and transport more Earth than their predecessors did just a decade ago. Yet ironically, the world's ultimate Earth mover is as old as the Earth itself. It's the raw power of Mother Nature. There's a tremendous amount of power that can be unleashed by storms or stream movement. It far exceeds our ability, what we can do as humans, to move soil in a rapid period of time. On the Gulf Coast of Mississippi, what took men and machines decades to build was destroyed in a matter of hours by one of the most destructive hurricanes on record. Hurricane Katrina made landfall in Mississippi on 29 August 2005, and it was a Category 4 hurricane, and the devastation was complete.
