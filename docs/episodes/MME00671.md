Behind them is the real business of keeping people out. Modern safes have been keeping thieves out of valuables for more than a century. Most rely on combination locks. But what happens if you lose that combination, and you're the one that's being kept out of your own safe? That's where eight-time world champion safecracker Jeff Sitar comes in. They open up safes and vaults for a living. And what I specialize in is what they call the art of manipulation, opening safes with just my hands, not drilling. Sitar uses sight, hearing, and feel to open anything from home safes to bank vaults. I've opened up a lot of bank vaults. My daily routine is cracking saves, and I've been doing this for 30 years. I practice 9 to 5, 5 to 6 days a week. This is what I do all day long. Other than his senses, the only tools Sitar uses are a magnifying glass to view the dial and an audio amplifier. We challenge Sitar to try to open a 60-ton bank vault in New Jersey that has a possible 100 million combinations. What we're standing in front of is a really beefed-up bank vault. It's a Remington Sherman, and it's probably from the 1930s. I mean, I don't know which number I'm going to get first. Each one reads differently. So I've got to find out the personality of the law, and then a plan of attack to open. Sitar knows how to attack any lock because he's spent years practicing. He keeps a collection of locks he's cracked in his house. I leave these locks around to brush up something or maybe I have a new idea on how to open or manipulate a safe open."*
