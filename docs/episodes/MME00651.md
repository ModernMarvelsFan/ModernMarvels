### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, measure it on Modern Marvels."*

> If you're wondering how far or how long, how heavy, how fast, or how cold, when you need to know what you're working with, you've got to measure it. From radar guns to atomic clocks, speedometers to whale scales, the high-precision tools that bear the heaviest loads, feel the heat, and respond to the slightest touch, allow us to build our civilization. Now, measure it on Modern Marvels. Your alarm clock erupts. It's chilly, so you crank up the thermostat before making breakfast. You flip on the news to see what the weather will be like outside. It's not even 8 a.m., and you've already encountered a series of measurements. and the rest of your day will be guided by an inescapable string of numbers that define your world. In your car, at the office, on the road, and even on the bathroom floor. Nearly everywhere you go and everything you do depends on measurements. They're what lifted us from the Stone Age into civilization. To ensure that we get the right amount of fruit, gas, or postage, the National Institute of Standards and Technology, or NIST, defines all of these measurements and more. We are responsible for what's known as realization of the primary units of measurement. We are charged with the research and the dissemination of the basic units by which we all measure our lives. NIST was born out of tragedies like the Great Baltimore Fire of 1904, which wouldn't have been such a disaster if all the United States were operating under a standardized set of measurements. During that fire, 1,500 buildings burnt to the ground. The Baltimore Fire Department called for help, and crews came from Washington, D.C. and New York City. But when they arrived in Baltimore City, they found that their hose couplings did not match the couplings of Baltimore fire hydrants. And so they were not able to help, and the importance of standards for things like hose couplings and fire hydrant fixtures became very, very apparent.
