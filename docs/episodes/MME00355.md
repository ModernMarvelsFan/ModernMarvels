### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, Engineering Disasters on Modern Marvels."*

> A drill bit leads to the disappearance of an entire lake and everything in it. The tallest man-made structures in the world turn to crumpled steel. A poorly designed dam wipes out an entire community in minutes. A freeway collapse in earthquake country. And the worst oil spill in US history. Now, Engineering Disasters on Modern Marvels. Architects and engineers have long been obsessed with the idea of building the tallest structure in the world. From the ancient pyramid of Khufu, which stood as man's highest creation for 43 centuries, before being eclipsed by the Eiffel Tower, to the 20th century's skyscrapers, which jostle for top rank as they push ever higher. The desire to be on top, looking down, is an enduring one. But all these magnificent structures fall far short of a stark utilitarian tower that rises up from the plains of North Dakota to a vertiginous height of 2,063 feet. Meet the KVLY Broadcasting Tower. Its antenna stands 356 feet higher than the tip of the antenna on the Sears Tower, higher than any other structure on Earth. They are very impressive structures and yet when you drive by them they don't look like much. In fact they're even hard to see under certain conditions. But there is an awful lot of very sophisticated engineering that goes into designing them. FM and television waves can't go around obstructions and thus require a very high launching pad to reach the desired coverage area. In regions of the Midwest and South lacking tall mountains, the solution is to build a tower of extreme height, like the KVLY tower.
