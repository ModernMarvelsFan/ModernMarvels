### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**

<details>
<summary><b>Click to view full opening narration (~2 minutes)</b></summary>

> *"Beyond the amber waves of grain, over a million dollars worth of equipment sits idle. These are the machines of the Meisner family, one of 700 crews, known as custom harvesters, who haul in nearly half of the United States wheat crop. Each year, they race against time to gather the delicate grain. Once the crop is ready, you need to be right on top of it, all within a week's time. and we actually follow the harvest of the wheat as it matures north. The responsibility of feeding the world weighs heavily on their shoulders. Any holdup at harvest could be catastrophic. At their stop in Elk City, Oklahoma, the Meisner crew has been delayed by a recent rainstorm and this custom harvester is desperate to get rolling again. When these machines of mine are not running, It's costing me $1,500 an hour. We've been sitting for a couple of days here, but we've had a good strong wind here, so hopefully this wheat is going to run now. But we'll just get out here and see what we got for a sample and check things out. Before harvest can begin, the moisture level of the wheat must be under 13.5 percent, any higher, and the wheat could spoil. The head, or spike of the wheat, contains the kernels, or grain, which are the only part of the plant used for human food. We're going to grab a few heads here, and the object is we're going to put them in my palm here, and we're going to thrash those kernels out of that head and then bite on them. And you can hear them crack, pop, it's going to be ready to go. We got a couple of soft ones, but it really tastes pretty darn good. We're going to go. We're going to try it. My crew's anxious to start cutting wheat, but we're going to get going here. Okay, Emma, get them organized. Rank them up. We're going to cut wheat. Copy the combines. Go ahead and raise all your heads up. Pull in and just make a nice gradual sweep."*
