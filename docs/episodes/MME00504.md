### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"We're about to fly into the eye of Hurricane Romo on Modern Marvels."*

> Their nature on a rampage. To be in their path is to be in the scariest place on Earth. The size and intensity of hurricanes make them the most feared and destructive of all storms. How do they start? How do we track them? Can we stop them? We're about to fly into the eye of Hurricane Romo on Modern Marvels. Now, Nature Tech Hurricanes on Modern Marvels. A swirling mass of clouds, ferocious winds, lightning, raging seas, torrential floods, hurricanes are the ultimate in extreme weather. In a typical strong hurricane, the energy budget of that hurricane releases on the order of tens of thousands of Hiroshima-sized nuclear weapons per second. Traveling for hundreds of miles and with potential wind speeds of over 200 miles per hour, they are capable of bringing nations to their knees. Those who follow and study them think of hurricanes almost as living, breathing entities. Every storm has a different personality and size matters. We have to know whether a storm is broad and destructive or very small and intense. They're capable of the virtual destruction of cities. On August 29, 2005, Hurricane Katrina triggered one of the worst natural disasters in the history of the United States. The tragedy left in the wake of this storm makes it more important than ever that scientists unlock the secrets of these destructive giants. Some of that learning starts here, with Hunter planes. These flying laboratories travel right through a hurricane and are equipped to collect information about the storm from the inside out. October 21st, 2005. This WC-130J is part of the Air Force Reserve's Weather Reconnaissance Squadron.
