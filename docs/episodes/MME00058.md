### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, American Steel, built to last on Modern Marvels, then and now."*

> Steel, for over a century, a symbol of America's might. Strong, durable, made to withstand great stresses. Steel created billionaires, a stock market explosion, vertical architecture, weapons of war, a strong labor union. Now, American Steel, built to last on Modern Marvels, then and now. By the 1950s, America was experiencing one of the greatest boons in the history of modern industry. Over 117 million tons of steel were produced in 1955, enough to make over 60 million automobiles. American steel was on top. Tens of thousands of workers made a handsome living in mills that were five, six, sometimes seven miles long. The incredible output of 100 million tons a year seemed almost easy. While the industry itself has undergone major changes over the years, the process of making steel has remained remarkably consistent. Iron ore gives us iron, and iron is what's used to make steel. The difference between iron and steel is the amount of carbon in each of them. Steel is the product that results if carbon can be driven out of the iron. The greatest challenge to making steel was finding out a way to remove carbon from iron. For over 5,000 years, civilizations have been struggling to unlock the secrets of iron and steel. The first use of iron really involved taking pieces of meteorites. People were able to shape these fragments, these pieces of material, this metal from heaven, and it fascinated people.
