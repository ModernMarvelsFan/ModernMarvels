### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, plane crashes on on Modern Marvels."*

> When the sophisticated machines that carry us near the edge of space fail, they do so horrifically, plummeting to Earth with a terrifying loss of life. But the truth is, passengers have never been safer because of the brightest minds, best technology, and billions of dollars focused on preventing the ultimate disasters. Now, plane crashes on on Modern Marvels. On July 18, 1996, TWA Flight 800 was en route from New York to Paris. A 747 with 230 passengers and crew, the plane was just south of island shoreline when something went catastrophically wrong. We were notified that an aircraft was missing, and then shortly thereafter we heard that boaters had found parts of the aircraft in the water that witnesses had seen an airplane go down. Over the next two years, investigators would piece together the puzzle of TWA 800 in hopes of averting future disasters and to bring reason to the senseless loss of life. But the emotional impact truth about these tragedies. They are extremely rare. Statistically, more people will die in one month of traffic accidents than in 40 years of jet travel. Governments, professional unions, and private companies endlessly struggle to keep the sky safe. Still, new tragedies elicit the dread or wound the human psyche as powerfully as the crash of a commercial jetliner. They strike at our most primal fears, heights, loss of control, and even death. They also call into question our dependence and vulnerability when the technology we trust with our lives fails. In the United States, understanding these failures is the job of the National Transportation Safety Board, or NTSB.
