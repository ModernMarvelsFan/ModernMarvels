Mechanical tribes stretching the technology of the 20th century, sometimes with disastrous results. Stories of adventure, daring, disaster, and dining. Oceanliners, now on Modern Marvels. The End Since the dawn of time, people have sailed the seas, a noble and daring enterprise with challenges to test the stern resolve of all who would explore new worlds. But by the mid-1800s, life on the high seas experienced a dynamic shift when the age of reason embraced one unreasonable desire, travel for the sheer pleasure of the traveling. It was called deep sea cruising, and it adopted a wholly new and elite aesthetic to make the journeys of life more important than the destinations. Certainly, passengers had places to go. But now, 30-day jaunts across the Atlantic could be elegant outings. What's more, the idea was considered healthful. In the 1880s, the British Medical Journal declared sea voyages curative. It was the steam-powered mechanical revolution which allowed this evolution to take place and permitted a ship's labor force to focus on the service of a passenger's comfort, rather than just the navigation of the vessel.
