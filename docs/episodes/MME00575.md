### 🎙️ Show Introduction

<details>
<summary><b>Click to view full opening narration (~2 minutes)</b></summary>

> *"My cell phone probably wouldn't flip open if it weren't for the Star Trek communicator. But Star Trek has much more exciting technology, like the warp drive and the transporter and the holodeck. And the question is, which of these can become real? In the last four decades, people all over this planet have come to know Star Trek as a science fiction staple. Its weekly fables of life and adventures in far-off galaxies have informed our imagination to what is possible and what is not. Set 300 years in the future, Star Trek is far enough away in time that the writers of the show could create ingenious ideas of travel, entertainment, and communication. After all, it was only 130 years ago that electricity became a reality, turning darkness into light. Since then, almost everything has changed in the modern world. That's why it's not hard for us to believe that one day we might be beaming ourselves from one place to another. You know, walking around campus on a beautiful fall day is great. But when you're late for class, it's a problem. It would be great to have a transporter. And the transporter is in fact what seduced me into writing about Star Trek. Because of all the fascinating physics I realized you'd have to think about if you wanted to move someone from one place to another almost instantaneously. Energize. The transporter is an ingenious storytelling device. As much as I travel, I would really enjoy being able to beam from one place to another. I'm interested in beaming, particularly. I'm really, I'm waiting on that. No more airplanes. You know what I'm talking about? I can just go over there. What's fascinating is imagining how something like this, if it were to be created, how it would change the world. I mean, imagine having a world where you didn't have half the place paved over for freeways and cars, and you people could just step into a machine and pop over here. With airline travel the way it is today, one would certainly wish it. On the other hand, you sometimes get what you wish for and you end up like the fly. I can still remember the day in Dr. Olofsson's transporter theory class when he was talking about the body being converted into billions of kiloquads of data"*

</details>
