> Run faster, jump higher, play longer. It's the ultimate goal of every athlete. But when training and preparation run their course, science steps up to the plate. It's a world where success comes not from the sweat of the player, but the brains of an engineer. Now, Sports Technology on Modern Marvels. In the fast-paced world of sports, the use of technology to enhance performance and improve safety has become common. Lighter materials have decreased the bulk and weight of equipment. Computer-aided design has pushed the limits of what was once thought impossible. For a particular athlete, we can look and say, you know, here's the shape of your arch, and we can make a shoe that fits precisely the way your foot is shaped and also how your foot moves. Both professional and amateur athletes have benefited from engineers coming up with new ways to improve sports. Runner's speeds increase, batted balls go farther, and the risk for many injuries has gone down dramatically. And a new kind of excitement enters a game when technology takes a swing. The same science that can catch speeders on a freeway can clock the velocity of a 100-mile-per-hour pitch. Radar guns have been used in the major leagues since the late 1980s, but now the radar baseball has arrived. What we've done is taking a traditional baseball and put computer technology in it that will actually tell you how fast you throw this when you throw it.
