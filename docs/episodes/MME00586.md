### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Engineering disasters of the 70s, now on Modern Marvels."*

> The 1970s, a time of great engineering feats. The CN Tower, the Sydney Opera House, and the Sears Tower. But also a time for engineering disasters. An apartment complex under construction collapses. The Edmund Fitzgerald disappears into Lake Superior. A dam breaks, wiping out an entire community. And 14 perish when an arena self-destructs. What were the flaws and reasons behind such destruction? Engineering disasters of the 70s, now on Modern Marvels. December 17th, 1976. A quiet evening at the Port of Los Angeles in San Pedro, California, was about to be shattered by an unexpected disaster. The Sansanina, a Liberian oil tanker, had just unloaded a cargo of 212,000 barrels of Indonesian crude. Her Italian crew then filled the empty cargo tanks with seawater. This is a routine practice called ballasting. It keeps the tanker riding low in the water on her voyage back at sea. About 7.35, somewhere along there in the evening, as I looked out that window, a tremendous pressure wave hit us. I thought somebody dropped the bomb. It sounded just like I looked out the window, and a big mushroom went up in the air. And I grabbed the fire phone, but it was dead. Everything had been cut off. The force of the blast could be felt 40 miles away. In response, some 200 firefighters raced to the pier. The first thing we saw when we arrived on the scene was a large mass of fire involving the wharf to such an extent that you couldn't identify what actually was out there. It appeared to me that there was a large building there on the wharf that was on fire, and yet I knew that there was no building there of that size. The building Dahlquist thought he saw was a chunk of the 70,000-ton San Sanina. Part of its midsection had blown out of the water
