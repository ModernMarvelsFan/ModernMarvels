### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, soft drinks on Modern Marvels."*

<details>
<summary><b>Click to view full opening narration (~2 minutes)</b></summary>

> *"They're the products of a multi-billion dollar industry that started out making cures for what ailed you, mass produced and microbrewed. We're the only people commercially who brew a soda that old fashioned way. That's our claim to fame. A tiny seed gives some of them more than twice the caffeine boost of a cup of coffee, while the key to formulating others is sending sweat through a centrifuge. It's time to twist your top, pour a glass, and chill out. Now, soft drinks on Modern Marvels. Nearly 15 billion gallons of soft drinks quench the thirsts of Americans each year. That's nearly the combined volume of coffee, beer, and bottled water consumed annually in the US. A host of flavors, eye-catching containers, and claims of invigoration continually entice us to gulp away. What exactly are soft drinks? Simply put, they're non-alcoholic liquid refreshments. But it's not quite that simple. Are coffees and teas soft drinks? No. Bottled water? Nope. Milk? Ditto. How about fruit juices? Maybe to some, but we'll save those for another show. I think in the United States, people tend to think of a soft drink more in the carbonated sense, so carbonated soft drinks. But in reality, it's broader than that. The carbonated soft drink category is far and away the largest beverage category in the US. People drink more carbonated soft drinks than any other beverage. U.S. soda makers produce enough carbonated soft drinks to supply each American with more than 50 gallons per year."*

</details>
