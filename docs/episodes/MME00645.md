<details>
<summary><b>Click to view full opening narration (~2 minutes)</b></summary>

> *"One animal that changed the course of civilization. A no-nonsense warrior. A beast of burden with strength, stamina, and speed. The heart of a multi-billion dollar industry. Give me three million to go. Do you have to get in three million? Do you have to get in three? And in some nations, the main course of an exotic meal. That looks really good. Now, the horse. on Modern Marvels. Horses. 75 million populate the earth today. 9 million in the United States. 350 different breeds, ranging from 30 inches to more than 6 feet tall. from 50 pounds to 2,500. Not predators but animals of prey. Instinctively motivated to flee from danger unless directed by a trustworthy human companion. Scientifically, horses may not be as smart as dogs or cats. It all comes down to brain size. On average, a horse's brain weighs only a pound, one thousandth or less of total body weight. Human brains weigh two to three pounds. Yet horse intelligence gives them extraordinary muscle balance and coordination, a combination that's earned them respect on racetracks, battlefields, farms, even city streets. You want a horse that's smart but not too smart. If they're too smart they try to figure out ways around whatever you're asking them to do. If they're not smart enough They never figure it out. They never learn. Fossil evidence shows the first animal considered a horse to be the much smaller condylarth, which roamed the earth 75 million years ago. It was the size of a fox terrier with actual toes. But during the course of evolution, the horse emerged with only one toe, called a hoof."*
