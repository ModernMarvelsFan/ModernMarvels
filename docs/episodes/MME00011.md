#### Cut B Opening Narration
> **Opening Hook:** *"Now, Dome Stadiums on Modern Marvels."*
>
> Domed roofs, the most organic space saving way to cover a stadium. In cities with hostile weather, they keep the big game excitement in and the unwanted elements out. Retractable roofs, the cutting edge in stadium design. They slide on rails, roll on wheels, or move on pulleys. These state of the art coverings allow teams to adopt to the climate in a matter of minutes, making weather delays a thing of the past. Now, Dome Stadiums on Modern Marvels. Because of cold weather and winter storms, baseball was only played in summer. But even then, searing heat and sudden showers could quickly cut games short. Likewise, other spectator sports, like football, could be seasonally disadvantaged. And the cost of building and maintaining large stadiums demanded year-round use. It was a very practical notion to consider an artificial environment to house big games from the elements. And it was a very American notion to accept the incredible scale which would be demanded in such a project. A dimension achieved in such accomplishments as Boulder Dam and the skyscrapers of Manhattan. The challenge of a structure's purpose often defines its design. As architect Louis Sullivan noted, form follows function. In putting a roof over a stadium, there would be no place to position support columns without disrupting the action of a game. The answer is an arched roof, which needs no columns because its weight is diverted laterally and supported by the building's walls. Those walls then need buttresses to keep from buckling under the increased load and further dissipate the overall weight. This is not a new idea. The concept has been used for centuries in the construction of Gothic cathedrals, where great height could not be accommodated through the support of columns. The modern approach, however, recalls earlier structures based on a dome design.
