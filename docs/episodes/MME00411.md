### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, Engineering Disasters on Modern Marvels."*

> A plumbing failure creates a sinkhole that can swallow a mansion. A dance party turns deadly when designer skywalks collapse. A dangerous cloud of gas explodes into a fiery industrial disaster. Dykes and levees give way to floodwaters that claim the lives of millions. And the miracle mineral turns out to be an invisible killer that suffocates thousands each year. Now, Engineering Disasters on Modern Marvels. Liquefied Natural Gas, or LNG, has become an increasingly popular energy choice for many nations. Though LNG has enjoyed a relatively safe track record, it can be an extremely dangerous commodity, capable of causing massive fires. LNG is natural gas in a liquid form. When natural gas is cooled to minus 259 degrees Fahrenheit, it becomes a clear, colorless, odorless liquid. By liquefying the natural gas, you reduce its volume by a factor of about 640, so you make it much, much smaller in volume, and it allows you to store it much more economically. This property of LNG allows for cost-effective transport of massive quantities of natural gas. Today, more than 150 LNG ocean tankers transport over 110 million metric tons of LNG annually to ports around the world. By 2010, LNG is projected to fulfill 10% of U.S. energy needs. There are currently 70 storage facilities called peak shaving plants in North America. They store LNG for peak demands. We also have five LNG import terminals and one export terminal in the U.S. By 2010, we probably will have five or six more import terminals. The concept of liquefying natural gas dates back to the 19th century.
