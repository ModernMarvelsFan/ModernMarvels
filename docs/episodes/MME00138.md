> *"It stands in Southern Egypt, one of the greatest engineering feats of the 20th century. The largest rock-filled dam in the world. Two and a half miles long, it harnesses the flow of the fabled River Nile. Now, the Aswan Dam on Modern Marvels. The Aswan High Dam, which controls the River Nile in southern Egypt, is the largest rock-filled dam in the world. Yet it is very difficult to get a good look at it. That's because it's almost impossible to determine where the land ends and the dam begins. In fact, you can be standing on it and not know it. The Aswan Dam is that big. Three years of Nile water is stored behind it. If released all at once, this water would sweep through the Nile Valley, potentially wiping out the entire country in a day. The superiority of this engineering is not only in its design, the concept of it, of its landfill, but also in its enormous size. It's a massive project, and indeed it is more massive than Sheop's pyramid at Giza in Cairo. The people of Egypt have a saying. The Nile is Egypt, and Egypt is the Nile. Since the beginning of recorded time, the Nile has been the source of life for the land through which it flows. This is true perhaps most of all in Egypt, a land of relentless desert where rain is sometimes not seen for a decade. The thousand mile course of the Nile through Egypt is a narrow valley of green never more than 12 miles wide until it reaches Cairo and broadens in the delta to meet the Mediterranean Sea."*
