#### Cut B Opening Narration
> **Opening Hook:** *"Now, plumbing the arteries of civilization on Modern Marvels."*
>
> Few industries are as big or as old or as crucial to human survival. From the dawn of civilization to the future of a small planet. Now, plumbing the arteries of civilization on Modern Marvels. If there is one fact of human life we all hold in common, it is our dependence on water. Regardless of race, creed, social strata, or historical era, we all rely on it for our very survival. Through all of its uses in modern daily life, the average American consumes more than 50 gallons of water per day. To reach the fixtures that place it at our fingertips, the water is often transported hundreds of miles from its source in the earth. Once it's reached the city, thousands of miles of underground water main carry the water into buildings, where it flows hot and cold at our beck and call. The art and science of plumbing is based on a fundamental law of nature. Water flows downhill. Like nature's rivers, man-made aqueducts are built on a downhill slope. using gravity to propel the water. The pressure in plumbing pipes is also created by gravity. Water is pumped to the top of giant towers which are connected to the mains beneath the streets. As long as the tower is higher than the water's final destination, gravity will maintain pressure and force the water through the pipes. In buildings that are taller than the water tower, water is pumped to a series of pressure tanks in the building, each one using gravity to feed the floors below it. After water is used, gravity once again carries it away. Sewage treatment plants are built at the lowest possible elevation, and sewer pipes are built at an angle so that wastewater can flow downhill.
