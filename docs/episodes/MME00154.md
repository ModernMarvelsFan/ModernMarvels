### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, plumbing on Modern Marvels."*

<details>
<summary><b>Click to view full opening narration (~2 minutes)</b></summary>

> *"It's civilization's pipe dream, surging with life-giving water and tons of waste, feeding the fixtures that spoil us and flushing us with pride. Its potential is explosive, and its history overflows with innovation. Now, plumbing on Modern Marvels. If there's one fact of life we all hold in common, it's our dependence on water. Regardless of race, creed, or social status, we all rely on it for our very survival. We've been depending on the craft and science of plumbing for thousands of years, as it enables us to bring fresh water where we need it and take dangerous wastewater away. Even though we kind of become complacent in the industry in so much as what we do every day, we kind of lose sight of the fact that the plumber is a very important part in the health industry. We don't have the outbreaks of diseases. We don't have any of the things that we had as little as a home years ago or less. Throughout history, the ability to control water has been one of the defining elements of a sophisticated culture. Plumbing is civilization. Just about every civilization that we look back on in antiquity that we study and admire for their contributions to art and literature and science, technology, civil order also were highly developed plumbers. Early concepts of plumbing, mere ditches in the ground, have developed into complex networks of pipes capable of supplying water to cities of millions. Ever wondered how much plumbing pipe you've got in your home? The average American house has over 700 feet,"*

</details>
