### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> Melty and magnificent, creamy and dreamy, whether it's mozzarella, manchego, American, or Swiss, when you take a snapshot of America's favorite foods, you got to say cheese. Last year, the average American ate a whopping 40 pounds of cheese. That's like taking two of the tires on your car, turning them into cheese, and then eating the tires. Last year, over 95% of American households bought cheese, which makes the cheese business bring in a whole lot of cheddar. Annual sales last year topped $34 billion. Annual production is around 13 billion pounds. That's almost equivalent to the weight of a million African elephants. Cheese makers around the country are working super hard to keep up with the demand, and I want to know how they do it. Just like fondue, we're going to immerse ourselves into all things cheese. I just want to go double-dutch with this for the rest of my life. From how it's made to where the most expensive cheese comes from, we'll learn how much mac and cheese we eat a year, the origins of string cheese, and why it peels the way it does. We'll even find out where Philadelphia cream cheese really comes from. And we've been granted access to a dark underground cave in the last place you'd ever expect it to find out how cheese is aged to moldy magnificence. This is Modern Marvel's The United States of Cheese. Today, I'm at Caputo Brothers Creamery, an artisan cheesemaker who has a great has been praised by Food & Wine magazine and some of the most prestigious restaurants in New York for their Italian-style mozzarella and ricotta. Caputo Brothers gets milk from local farms and turns it, or maybe I should say churns it, into 6,700 pounds of cheese every week. That's 350,000 pounds of cheese per year.
