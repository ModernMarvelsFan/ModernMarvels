### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"It's built to last on Modern Marvels."*

> From the unthinkable... Our recorders survive in the worst of crashes. ...to the unimaginable... There is nothing that this cannot protect you from. They're meant to survive, even if we don't. They could be here for centuries, even though we use them every day, and outlast us long into the afterlife. Metal caskets will last years into the future. From eternity to liberty, some things just have to endure. Stick around. It's built to last on Modern Marvels. Whether it's structures expected to endure through the ages, or devices we count on when we need them most, few things on Earth are truly built to last. But perhaps the very symbol of long-lasting innovation is found inside one of man's greatest inventions. Whether you're a fan of the friendly skies or afraid to fly, most of us try not to dwell try not to dwell on the idea of a plane crashing. But for the experts at L3 Communications, it's a threat they think about every day. At their headquarters in Sarasota, Florida, the mission is to make sure at least part of the plane, the black box, will survive a potentially catastrophic event. The crash survival tests are meant to simulate an actual crash event of an airplane. L3 engineers manufacture and test these icons of survival. Large passenger planes contain not one, but two of the widely recognized devices. In commercial air carriers you'll find both a cockpit voice recorder, like this shorter box, and a flight data recorder, like this longer box. The first thing about black boxes that's clear is they're not black at all.
