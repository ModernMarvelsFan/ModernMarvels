<summary><b>Click to view full opening narration (~2 minutes)</b></summary>

> *"It arrives from outer space, blows in with the wind, rides in with the waves, and rises from the molten core of the Earth, just in time to save a planet in crisis. Earth, air, fire, water, and imagination. Limitless resources whose time has come. Now, renewable energy on Modern Marvels. The action never stops at the FedEx Regional Sort Facility. Located at Oakland International Airport in California, it's the shipping giant's hub for the Pacific region. 1,700 workers and a state-of-the-art conveyor system sort about 85 million packages per year. There's more than a quarter of a million packages that transit that transit this facility in a given day. And we, in a month, are using approximately 450,000 to 500,000 kilowatt hours of power. To meet these electricity demands, FedEx decided to build its own power plant. It's silent, has no moving parts, and it's located conveniently on the roof. Taking advantage of abundant sunshine and 81,000 square of unused roof space, FedEx hired Sharp Solar to design this photovoltaic electricity system in 2005. Today, it supplies 80% of the facility's peak electricity needs. We're looking at a system here that's 904 kilowatts, a very large system. It might be the largest system, rooftop system, on a commercial building in the state of California. And it's composed of about 5,700 solar modules."*
