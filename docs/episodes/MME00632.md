### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"From colliding asteroids to subatomic particles, Now, crashes on Modern Marvels."*

> 3,000 pounds of race car out of control. You're going to lose it. You're going to crash. You're going to spin. Motorcycles crumple in high-speed smash-ups. A woman hits the Earth from over 10,000 feet with a failed parachute. I was certain I was going to die from that impact. From colliding asteroids to subatomic particles, Now, crashes on Modern Marvels. High speeds. Close formations. Split-second maneuvers. Yellow fly, yellow fly, yellow. All the things that make auto racing so thrilling also make it deadly. Actor-turned-race car driver Frankie Muniz knows just how dangerous the sport can be. Being a race car driver, you know you're going to be involved in accidents every once in a while, big crashes and stuff. It's kind of something that comes with the territory of being a driver. You know it's going to happen, and most of the time you can't avoid it, and they hurt. Last season, I actually fractured my back, broke my wrist, broke my ankle, broke six ribs. So I've had a few big ones. One of the primary causes for auto racing crashes is driver error. The driver's always trying to find the limit on the car. How far can I brake? How fast can I take this corner? Can I go a little bit faster? And sometimes you just take it a little bit too fast, And the car just doesn't have the grip, can't take that speed, and you will lose control of the car and hit the wall. On average, there's one serious injury for every 300 racing accidents. But 40 years ago, that ratio was 1 in 10. When I started IndyCar racing in the late 60s, we lost a guy a weekend. There's a lot of fire deaths.
