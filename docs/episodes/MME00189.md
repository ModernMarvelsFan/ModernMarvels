### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, quarries on Modern Marvels."*

> Hill sides explode. Massive blocks of stone break off of solid rock walls. And powerful machines crush large rocks in a matter of seconds. Now, quarries on Modern Marvels. These solid granite walls are hundreds of feet thick and seemingly impenetrable. But they don't protect a high-level security building for the government or an underground testing site. These walls belong to Rock of Ages Quarry located in Barrie, Vermont. The company is the largest granite quarrier in North America. Its haul? Over 118,000 tons of rock a year, atop one of the richest deposits of granite ever discovered. And this quarry boasts some of the most powerful equipment in the world. Its drills cut through solid rock at over three feet per minute. Once free of the wall, huge trucks shuttle the 200-ton granite blocks to towering derricks, Trains that hoist them cautiously to the surface, 60 stories up. Quarrymen toil doggedly year-round in brutal, ever-changing weather conditions, chipping away at the rock face. This mammoth upheaval of granite stretches for miles across the landscape. Like an iceberg, only its tip is exposed. Its mass extends more than 10 miles below the surface. This granite deposit, like every other type of rock, was formed from magma, molten matter from the Earth's core. Over 500 million years ago, magma, or igneous rock, sluggishly oozed its way toward the surface under tremendous pressure, eventually cooling. Geologists classify rock in three categories, igneous rock, metamorphic, and sedimentary rock.
