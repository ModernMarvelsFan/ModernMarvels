### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, the world's longest bridge on Modern Marvels."*

> Two and a half miles long, a thousand feet high, and 120,000 tons of high-strength steel. Challenged by typhoon and earthquake, it's the longest, heaviest, and most expensive suspension bridge ever built. Now, the world's longest bridge on Modern Marvels. It's one of the most violent waterways in the world. Wrecked by typhoons, cursed with driving seas, and with currents reaching 15 feet per second, it's the Akashi Strait in Japan's inland sea. Long a major barrier to uniting northern and southern Japan, This two-and-a-half-mile watery divide is now spanned by the mightiest bridge in the world. The most technologically advanced suspension design ever built, the Akashi-Kaikyo Bridge. The Japanese were presented with a very interesting challenge in the Akashi Straits. It's a relatively narrow strait. It's a major shipping channel. 1,400 commercial vessels that pass through the straits every single day. Coupled with that, then, is the currents or the flow of water through the Akashi Straits. The Akashi Channel was a major challenge to Japan's bridge builders. The strait is over 360 feet deep. The intense currents that course through it cause violent flows at the surface and rapid erosion on the seabed. It is crowded with both fast-moving freight and stationary fishing vessels. And in the center of this, the engineers propose to situate two nearly 1,000-foot-high bridge towers and join them with a record-breaking 12,828-foot span, which would link north to south. Economic development is very important to the Japanese, and of course the populated areas of Tokyo and Osaka have the population density. The Japanese want to open up for economic development many of the other parts of their country.
