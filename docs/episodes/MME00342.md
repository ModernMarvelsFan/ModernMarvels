### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, ET Tech, the scientific search for extraterrestrial life on Modern Marvels."*

> When we finally encounter an extraterrestrial life form, will we study it under a microscope? Or get to shake hands? Either way, it's bound to change our lives. Now, ET Tech, the scientific search for extraterrestrial life on Modern Marvels. In Puerto Rico, the world's largest radio telescope aims at a distant star. Scientists monitor it around the clock, hoping to detect an artificially generated radio signal, evidence of an extraterrestrial civilization. At the Lick Observatory near San Jose, California, astronomers search the heavens for a high-powered light beacon, a calling card beamed by extraterrestrials from a solar system trillions of miles from Earth. Meanwhile, four NASA space probes launched in the 1970s, carrying information about our planet, continue their journey to worlds far beyond our solar system. These are a few of the ways that astronomers have been searching for intelligent extraterrestrial life, or E.T. himself. But just what do these searchers consider intelligent? If you can build a radio transmitter or a high-powered laser so that we can detect you, then from our point of view, you're intelligent. While encountering intelligent life might be the pinnacle moment in human history, scientists are no less eager to encounter extraterrestrials that can only be seen under a microscope. Let's say if we go to Mars and we find evidence of microbial life, there are actually two things that could mean. One is that life on Mars and Earth are related. Somehow they're products of the same event. And as time went by, Mars could no longer sustain life. And so we're looking, maybe looking at ancient distant cousin. The other possibility is it started of its own origin, not of Earth, but of Mars itself. Now that has the possibility of giving us a whole other example of what life is like.
