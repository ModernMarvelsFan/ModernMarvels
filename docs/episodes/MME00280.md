> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, the Army Corps of Engineers on Modern Marvels."*

> They build battlements and bridges, roads and reservoirs. They are the largest public engineering concern in the world. They have shaped and reshaped the face of our continent. And they've helped boost us into outer space. Now, the Army Corps of Engineers on Modern Marvels. On July 26, 2001, in an act that seemed to defy the laws of nature, a massive 11,000-ton piece of concrete floated 27 miles up the Ohio River to a final destination along the Monongahela River near Pittsburgh. At 333 feet long, 104 and 1 half feet wide, and 50 feet high, this portion of the new Braddock Dam, which is bigger than a football field, was the largest cast piece of concrete and rebar ever to set sail. This daring feat of engineering bravado was conceived, designed, and executed by the United States Army Corps of Engineers. Built along the banks of the Ohio River, the giant slab of concrete marks the first time that a dam has been prefabricated in one place, then moved by water to another. But how to get a piece of concrete this massive to float? The answer lay in a simple yet elegant design feature. The dam's sections were designed with large empty baffles inside, which aided in flotation during the nautical journey.
