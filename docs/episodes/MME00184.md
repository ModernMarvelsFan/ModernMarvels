### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, Cattle Ranches on Modern Marvels."*

> It's a legendary part of the American past, the job that created the cowboy. It's the heart of the food chain, the business of eating and being eaten. It's as old as farming and as current as cloning, an industry in which a single bull can sire a hundred thousand calves. Now, Cattle Ranches on Modern Marvels. The image of cattle ranching in America is still the traditional one, full of open spaces, big skies, large herds, and cowboys. But cattle ranching today isn't really about cowboys and countryside. It's about meat. The very serious and intensely competitive business of putting some 25 billion pounds of steak, barbecue and hamburger on the national grill each year. Never have the economic stresses and demands been so great. Never have ranchers been forced to speed up and streamline the very cycles of nature to push their cattle quite so hard, to grow them so big, so fast, so meaty, so uniform, using everything from sonograms that analyze the genetic quality of an animal's flesh to computer chips that carry growth and weight statistics stretching back for generations to fertility pills and embryo transplants that enable a single cow to conceive as many as 36 calves. All this in an effort to make cattle ranching as efficient as an assembly line and to woo consumers with consistently tender stakes. In cattle ranching, the Old West is butting up hard against the 21st century bottom line. Used to be you'd put a cow and bull together, have a calf, sell a calf, make money. It's not like that anymore. It's pretty high-tech. You've got to do it correctly or you're out of business.
