### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, Taxidermy on Modern Marvels."*

<details>
<summary><b>Click to view full opening narration (~2 minutes)</b></summary>

> *"The ones closely kept secrets of body preservation. Now, Taxidermy on Modern Marvels. Each year in Alaska, the Department of Fish and Game grants an average of 700 permits to hunt Kodiak bears in an effort to control the population. He's big. He's over 9 foot. The fee is $500, but Joe Coolis of Bedford, Ohio has spent an additional $5,000 in expenses to take his shot. Take him now, Joe. He's down. He's hit hard. He's on his back in a hole. Coolis isn't here to bring food back for the family table. He's only after the skin and pelt. With at least two additional bears circling the kill, Kulis must quickly skin and flesh the Kodiak's hide. The massive bundle of fur now begins a remarkable journey that will take it first to the tanner, where the skin is salted, pickled, and turned into a soft, nearly permanent leather. Then it's shipped to Kulis' own taxidermy shop and studio. Here it's fitted over a foam form and glass eyes. The finished mount is an uncanny match for the bare coolest bagged in the frozen north. And now you have a life-size photograph, so to speak. You can look and say, oh, I remember when that happened and this happened. And then a lot of times it's definitely dangerous. So I think it's the accomplishment of what you have to go through and then achieving something, then reproducing it so it looks exactly like it did when alive. You put all those things together and now you have something and think, wow, that was really great. Very few people are neutral when confronted with a once living head hanging from a wall, or a giant predator frozen at the moment of attack. Many find the ancient practice of mounting animals for display disturbing and downright macabre."*

</details>
