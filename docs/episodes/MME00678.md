### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"We now return to supersized food on Modern Marvels."*

> It's big. It's bold. It's what's for dinner. Go big or go home. As food becomes cheaper and more available, America's favorite dishes have grown bigger and bigger. And today, specialty butchers, bakers, and candy makers are serving them biggest of all. From huge hot dogs to colossal cupcakes, it takes vats of raw material, newly designed tools of the trade, and the strength of many to mix, bake, and serve such king-sized bears. This is insane. Now, it's super-sized food on Modern Marbles. You guys ready to eat? Yeah! Yeah! No matter what they say, let's face it, Size matters. Bigger is better. And nowhere is this culture of big more apparent than in the United States. We are a land of super-sized appetites, super-sized resources, and super-sized egos. It's one of the great things about being an American. It's very Darwinian, maybe, you know what I mean? So people with the biggest stuff are somehow going to be able to end up better in the end and survive and send off offspring to have big stuff as well. From sea to shining sea, we've supersized our homes, our vehicles, our electronics, and now our food. Eat for me now! One of the things in the United States that's enabled us, that's afforded us this ability to be a supersizing country ends up being there's tremendous availability and tremendous affordability of food. It's cheaper, it's unbelievably convenient. We don't have to go kill the buffalo to have a burger. Serving sizes of our favorite foods have been growing for decades. When McDonald's first opened in 1955, it served up just one size burger. A meat patty, a mere 1.6 ounces.
