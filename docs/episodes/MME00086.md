### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, mapmaking on Modern Marvels."*

> Plotting distant planets and your own neighborhood. Please proceed to the highlighted route. Maps fueled by adventure. Older than recorded history. Now, mapmaking on Modern Marvels. A map was there when man first walked on the moon. William Clark drew this map when he charted America's Northwest Passage with Meriwether Lewis. This is the planet Venus. It's also a map. Throughout history, maps have inspired adventure and warned of danger. They've been created as great works of art and to plot horrific carnage. They've also helped us get to where we're going. In addition to geography, the map itself gives you an idea of what the technology was at the time that the map was constructed. We have maps in the past where there weren't great cartographic techniques and tools, and as a consequence, maps tended to be very simple, simply because people could not create a complex image. Today, with our advances in computer science and computer graphics, we can create very, very specific displays with great deals of complexity. No place are the technological advances of cartography better demonstrated than in New York City. During the 20th century, New York generated an incomparable energy stoked by the wonder of skyscrapers, the power of Wall Street, and the ever-present fear of crime. Violence tarnished the city's greatness. In the late 1990s, however, violent crime began to plummet.
