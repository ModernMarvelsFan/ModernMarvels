> *"Simply boil water to see what it can do. Provide locomotion, generate electricity, clean just about anything, and power outlandish gizmos. But if you don't watch out, it can huff and puff and blow your house down. All right, ready to go balls out, ready to go full steam ahead? You'll find out where all these terms come from. Coming up. Now, Steam on Modern Marvels. Steam cars. In the early days of the automobile, they were more popular than the internal combustion engine. To learn about them from an expert, we went to Jay Leno's garage. In two enormous buildings, Jay has amassed a collection of exceptional and unusual motor vehicles, including some classic steamers. What we're going to do right now is fire up a 1909 Stanley steam car. Steam cars were quite popular at the turn of the century because, don't forget, cars back in the early days, you had to crank them, and you had a magneto, and you had to set the choke. And a lot of times, they'd backfire, and people would break their arms. Where steam, well, steam technology was 100 years old by the time this car was built. Steam technology was easily understood. You heat up water, it makes steam, it pushes the piston, and you go. Let's open up the hood. OK, not as pretty as looking at a 289 Mustang or 327 Chevy, but this is not your engine. This is just your boiler. Getting an old steamer ready to run isn't simple. It's a multi-step, time-consuming process. And firing up the boiler can be an adventure. This is one of the few cars you can get burned to death and scalded to death at the same time. I mean, you're carrying an open flame, and you're made of wood. Adding air pressure? Easy."*
