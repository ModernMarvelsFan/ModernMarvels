### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, Bricks on Modern Marvels."*

> A story as old as civilization. A simple alchemy of earth, water and fire. A timeless handicraft that has progressed to a mass production miracle. A durable building block. And literally, the brick and mortar of great cities. Now, Bricks on Modern Marvels. Towering 450 feet into the sky is Western Kentucky Energy's newest chimney. From the outside, it may look like just another concrete structure. However, inside the concrete shell is a crucial 30-foot diameter brick lining. Western Kentucky Energy's chimney liner will require nearly 350,000 bricks, 210,000 pounds of mortar, and the sweat of the bricklayers that put it all together. Each brick weighs approximately 11 pounds, and each bricklayer lays about 1,000 bricks a day, so during the course of a day he's lifting approximately 11,000 pounds. Get you right in here and get you right in here. It just takes a lot of time and practice. I mean, if you do it every day, every day you get better and better at it. It's kind of exciting being up this high, up in the air. The challenge of the job itself, you can say that you've done something at the end of the job. Each and every day. The brick will protect the concrete from the caustic gases exhausted from the daily operation of the power plant. Older brick chimneys were constructed with outer brick walls and inner brick liners. The outer wall was a common brick or radial brick without acid-resistant qualities. Concrete has replaced brick on the exterior, but the internal wall still needs to be an acid-resistant material.
