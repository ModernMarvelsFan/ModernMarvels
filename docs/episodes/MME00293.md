### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> For over six years, it was one of the best kept secrets in motorcycling. A Harley that was unlike anything Harley had ever built. When it was finally unveiled in the summer of 2001, the new bike came with a long list of firsts. Harley's first liquid-cooled engine, its first exposed steel and aluminum body, aluminum body, the first Harley to do 115 horsepower straight out of the box. They called it the V-Rod. It all started with the idea of doing something that had never been done before. For almost a hundred years, Harley Davidson had put out some of the most classic motorcycles ever built. In many ways, they became a symbol of the American spirit itself. By the 1990s, Harleys were so popular that you needed to be on a waiting list just to buy one. Despite this success, Harley was losing ground to a new generation of riders, more interested in speed than in style. At a meeting of Harley's product development committee, the idea began to take shape. Was it possible to combine the best of both worlds? The speed and power of a performance bike wrapped up in a style that was unmistakably Harley. The top secret project was codenamed P4. We were really interested in reaching out to an even broader base of customers. Everybody had their own concept in what this thing was going to be, but we knew what the essence of this motorcycle was going to be. Bare bones, powerful, custom. To get the performance we wanted and the acceleration we wanted and all the things we wanted out of this motorcycle, we realized we had to do something new.
