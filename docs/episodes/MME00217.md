### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, Siege Machines on Modern Marvels."*

> From time immemorial, humans have been developing weapons that can blast through, break down, and breach the world's strongest fortifications. From the ancient battering ramp to medieval catapults and crossbows, from cannons and tanks to computer-guided cruise missiles. Now, Siege Machines on Modern Marvels. King Edward had one. Napoleon had one. And so does Hugh Kennedy. Early on in my youth, I read about these large siege machines, and I was rather more than fascinated by the idea of building one sometime, particularly when I heard that the emperor Napoleon III had tried to build one in the 1850s and had not really succeeded very well. Hugh Kennedy, a wealthy English landowner and military historian, owns the only full-size trebuchet, a giant seesaw-like device in existence today. A trebuchet is an engine of war. It's used for throwing large weights. In the late 12th and early 13th centuries, siege machines similar to this four-story, 30-ton instrument instrument of destruction, were used to demolish fortress walls at will. The biggest ones were capable of throwing a rock maybe weighing 600 pounds. And with a rock of that size, you can do quite a bit of damage. The trebuchet was fearsome because it was repeatable and it had awesome power. The most powerful thing, the most powerful machine on the planet at the time. And we do have instructions drafted in the late 13th century
