### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, Levees on Modern Marvels."*

> It's man versus nature in a battle to hold back water. Massive earthen structures hundreds of miles long and monumental feats of engineering know-how. Vitally important, though often taken for granted. Until disaster strikes. Now, Levees on Modern Marvels. The images are all too familiar. Flooding on a mass scale. Staggering property damage. Human suffering. And death. eventually a storm was going to overwhelm those levees in new orleans those levees lost the battle of too much water too fast when katrina decimated new orleans hurricane defense system levees became tragic symbols for what can go wrong when we think we can outsmart mother nature there are two kinds of levees those that have failed and those that will fail and anybody who tells you they can engineer fail-safe levees is not giving you the whole truth. The forces that came together to produce the flood disaster in New Orleans are vastly complex. But levees themselves are relatively simple in design and purpose. Whether made of soil, stone, or steel, levees are built to hold back water. The most common levees are massive earthen mounds. As people began developing areas near rivers, They just piled up dirt to defend against those rivers. And that process is what we call a levee. So a levee, fundamentally, is a pile of dirt. To withstand the kind of extreme pressure water can exert, levees need to be ten times as wide as they are high. The point at which the base of the levee meets the land, or river bottom, is called the toe.
