### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, Money on Modern Marvels."*

> Cold hard cash. Whether it jingles in our pockets or folds in our wallets, it flows by the billions from government agencies that have mastered the craft of making it. Empowering consumers and confounding counterfeiters. The result of time-honored technology, painstaking artistry, and precision automation. Now, Money on Modern Marvels. They say that it can't buy happiness. And that the love of it is the root of all evil. But most of us seem to be obsessed with earning, saving and spending it. For better or worse, money is the measure of all things in America. And two agencies work around the clock, satisfying our demand for it. The Bureau of Engraving and Printing produces our paper currency. And the United States Mint, the largest mint in the world, manufactures our coins. We are the largest mint in large measure because we have the world's largest economy. Many mints worldwide have had to slow their production down. In fact, some have had to shut their production down. We, on the other hand, because of the size of our economy, have had very smooth production capacity. The U.S. Mint runs production facilities in Denver, San Francisco, and West Point. But for more than two centuries, the heart of its operation has been in downtown Philadelphia. You can count on this money-making factory to pump out a staggering 11 billion coins each year. On an average day, we make over 30 million coins. About half of them are pennies, and the rest are split between nickels, dimes, and quarters. No parts of the Mint's automated production line are more critical than the dyes from which the coins are stamped. Creating them is a meticulous and time-consuming challenge. The process begins with drawings crafted by the Mint staff of sculptor engravers.
