### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, Vacuums on Modern Marvels."*

> Nature may abhor a vacuum, but we seem to love them. They clean our floors and deliver medicine. They've got the suction power needed by industry. They're first on the scene at catastrophic sites. And they battle algae that's destroying coral reefs. They even cut our hair. From little suckers to industrial giants. Now, Vacuums on Modern Marvels. Ground Zero, 9-11. Hurricane Katrina. Plane crashes, train wrecks, catastrophes on a monstrous scale. When calamity strikes, who cleans up? Tradewind's environmental restoration in Holtzville, New York, has become the company to call for mop-up after disaster. The single most important tool of their trade? Vacuums. I can't imagine a business that uses vacuums more than we do. It would be very difficult for us to do any kind of cleaning without vacuums. And that's almost across the board, whether it's hazardous waste or controlled waste or even just after fires and floods. After the collapse of the World Trade Center towers, Tradewinds was on site from day one. At Ground Zero, we needed vacuums. The reason why we needed the vacuums was due to the fact that we had 210 stories that collapsed. We had drywall dust. We had cement dust. We had asbestos. We had insulation. It all made up of dust that we never had before on a disaster site. The fallout was up to your kneecaps and some of the buildings. Tradewinds employed over 2,000 workers and hundreds of heavy-duty vacuums to clean the toxic dust from over 4 million square feet of building space, the equivalent of 70 football fields.
