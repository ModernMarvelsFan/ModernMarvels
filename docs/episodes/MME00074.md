### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Join me up next on Modern Marvels, firefighting containing the demon."*

> It's nature's most primal force, and the force we fear the most. Every year, fire kills nearly 5,000 Americans. The history of firefighting is a tale of courage and innovation. I'm John Travolta from the new movie, Ladder 49. Evacuate now! I'm not leaving until you leave! One of our guys went missing, Jack Morrison, Ladder 49. Get in there and find him. Join me up next on Modern Marvels, firefighting containing the demon. Paramedic 1, I need the address of the problem. What's burning? Do you actually see smoke or flames? It happens every 16 seconds somewhere in America. The alarm, the sirens, and a race toward the unknown. Most people in their lifetimes may only see a few fires, and so they don't think of it as occurring very frequently. But in fact, it does. There are 2 million fires a year in the United States. At most every blaze is the risk of death. Why is it so dangerous? The unknown, mostly. You don't know when the roof's going to cave in on you, when those beams are going to start blowing. You never know when something's going to go wrong. You do get the feel of the adrenaline. You're put in a position where the heat is there and the smoke's over your head. And you have to get together with your teammate and say, okay, I think we can make it. We're going to go on down there, and we're going to put the wet stuff on the red stuff when lose or draw. To make firefighting safer and more effective, there's an arsenal of high-tech equipment. Mobile computers provide instant blueprints of major buildings.
