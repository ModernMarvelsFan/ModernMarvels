### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, Times Square on Modern Marvels."*

> It's where America has celebrated victory and rung in the new year. It's the entertainment and the advertising capital of the world. Throughout its history, it has exuded glamour, sleaze, progress, and excitement. Now, Times Square on Modern Marvels. Time Square, December 31st, 1999. An estimated 2 million people are jammed into the crossroads of the world, as it has long been called, with billions more watching on TV around the world. It's a national party. it's hard to imagine taking place on the same scale with the same emotion anywhere else. There's nothing like this. This is New Year's Eve. You're witnessing the passing of time, the moment when one year ends and the next year begins. And we've always said, you know, all we have to do is drop the ball. That's the main thing. We don't promote this event. People come. New Year's Eve may not be promoted, but for the millennium at least, it was produced like a Broadway extravaganza, complete with pageantry, five tons worth of confetti, and a mid-Manhattan fireworks display. We fire off pyro from two buildings on either side of the venue, as well as One Time Square, which is where the ball drop actually happens, down on the bookend. One Times Square itself is a 300-plus foot building, and we probably go another 150 to 250 feet off of that, depending on the various shots. Total number of shots, my guess, is probably in the range of 1,000 to 1,500 shots. With tricky wind currents created by so many high-rises, to say nothing of the huge crowd, the explosives must be launched with extreme care. Fireworks that burn out long before they hit the ground, called cold burn pyro, make this display possible.
