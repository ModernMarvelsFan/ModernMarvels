### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, Balls on Modern Marvels."*

> They're engineered with the utmost seriousness and manufactured with unprecedented precision. All in the pursuit of play. But whether hit, rolled, slammed, or driven to extremes, they always bounce back for more. Now, Balls on Modern Marvels. Balls. They spin through our lives in predictable ways, so much so that we take their performance for granted. But the fuzzy, pebbled, gleaming, dimpled surfaces on most modern balls conceal an impressive history of research and development. One ball, however, is a bit of a throwback. Befitting its smash-mouth sport, the football is forged from the basics. Cowhide, sweat, and perseverance. At Wilson's Ada, Ohio plant, most of the employees are veterans who have been on the job for at least two decades. Cutting, stitching, turning, and lacing footballs for the National Football League. But they make more than just NFL balls. We make them for colleges, high schools, we make them for the youth league, whether it would be midget, tiwi, or the junior high. We make between 3,000 and 5,000 balls here every day, Monday through Thursday. Somewhere around 800,000 balls will be made this year. The plant will produce about 30,000 balls for the NFL each year, a little less than 1,000 for each of the 32 teams. A pro ball begins with cowhide that has been dyed, pressed into a pebbled grain for gripping, and treated with a resin-based material to give it a tacky texture. Still, quarterbacks feel the need to rough up a new ball. When you get a football and it's brand new, it comes with a kind of a slick film. You just want to take it and throw it into the ground a couple times and just rub it
