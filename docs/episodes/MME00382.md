> *"It is known as technology's angel of death, but it might be humanity's savior, able to generate a nearly limitless supply of power. Technology sired by splitting the atom has led to an incredible array of innovations. Now, Nuclear Tech on Modern Marvels. 3, 2, 1, and liftoff of the Cassini spacecraft on a trillion-mile trek to Saturn. When the Cassini spacecraft launched in 1997, its goal was to journey to Saturn, about 890 million miles from Earth. But providing power for such a long journey called for inventive measures. Conventional chemical propulsion would make the craft too heavy. and because Saturn is so far from the sun, solar power would be ineffective. But there was another choice, nuclear technology, in the form of a radioisotope thermoelectric generator. The radioisotope thermoelectric generator produces electricity continuously over a long period of time, many decades. And it produces it in the absence of sunlight sunlight and is a wonderful, stable source of electricity to keep our spacecraft running in the cool, dark heaps of space, basically. The Cassini spacecraft carries three radioisotope thermoelectric generator units. These RTGs take advantage of an isotopic decay process. Isotopes over time decay by releasing alpha particles. The process also gives off energy in the form of heat. When this happens, matter is converted to energy. It's Einstein's famous equation, E equals mc squared, come to life. Energy released equals the mass that is converted to energy times the speed of light squared. Cassini's thermoelectric modules"*
