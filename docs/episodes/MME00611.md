### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now fast food tech, BillionServed on Modern Marvels."*

> From San Diego to Sweden, they churn out food for the masses. What type of combo would you like? Where seconds equal dollars, tech innovation rules, and flipping burgers is a thing of the past. These industry giants grow it, process it, freeze it, ship it, track it, fry it, and pack it. All so we can pay them to eat it. Have a great day. You too. Now fast food tech, BillionServed on Modern Marvels. Lunch rush at a busy McDonald's in Oak Brook, Illinois. In two hours, about 40 employees will serve as many as 550 meals. Worldwide, in over 31,000 locations, McDonald's serves 50 million customers each day. On an annual basis, we serve over 800 million pounds of beef. We serve over 600 million eggs, all of that on top of the fact that we serve over 3 billion pounds of our world-famous French fries. With eateries in well over 100 countries, McDonald's reigns as the undisputed champion of fast food ubiquity. But it's not easy to please a world full of culturally varied palates. McDonald's business is really largely based on a core menu product, which would be Big Macs and burgers and fries and shakes. However, occasionally in international markets, we'll go out and develop products that are really developed specifically for a local taste. Examples of that may be the Maharaja Mac, which is served in India, which is actually a chicken-based version of the Big Mac. Things like the Teriyaki Burger, which is a pork-based product that's served in Japan. and various rice products that are served in other places across Asia. If you live in the Western world, odds are good you've eaten McDonald's food at least once. And if you're like most Americans, you probably eat some kind of fast food at least once a week. In fact, over 75% of all restaurant visits happen at a fast food restaurant.
