> *"Possibility at the height of the Cold War. How to stop it? The heart of the answer lies inside this mountain. It's a high-tech, bomb-proof, underground command center coordinating the largest array of military defense systems ever created. Now, NORAD, the electronic sentinel, on Modern Marvels, then and now. High above Colorado Springs in the front range of the Rockies stands a 9,565-foot citadel of solid granite. It's Cheyenne Mountain. Carved out of solid rock, a third of a mile below the surface in a man-made grid of chambers and tunnels designed to withstand a thermonuclear strike is the Cheyenne Mountain Operations Center. Behind these hardened glass doors that weigh nearly 25 tons is a four and a half acre subterranean compound that is the largest and most complex command and control center in the world. Over 1200 men and women including civilians work in Cheyenne Mountain. The Cheyenne Mountain complex was not only built for self-sufficiency and security it was literally built to rock and roll. The 15 buildings that make up the complex sit atop hundreds of three-foot-high steel springs. These 1,000-pound steel coils were designed to absorb the seismic shockwaves of a thermonuclear strike or an earthquake. Since 1966, the mountain has been the operation center for NORAD, the North American Aerospace Defense Command, and since 1985, America's newest military service, the U.S. Space Command. NORAD continues to carry out a mission that began during the darkest days of the Cold War, when our national leaders feared the possibility of a surprise nuclear attack from the Soviet Union. NORAD's mission is the air sovereignty, aerospace warning and aerospace control for North America. And since the Gulf War, Cheyenne Mountains' mission now includes the protection of American and Canadian forces around the world."*
