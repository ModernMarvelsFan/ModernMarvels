### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Next, City Parks on Modern Marvels."*

> Anchoring opposite coasts of America are three of the world's most visited and beloved parks. From the forlorn stretch of beach that's now San Francisco's green and glorious Golden Gate Park. This is a landscape that was changed almost 98%. To Griffith Park, given to the city of angels by a man who is anything but. People recognize that he's a leader, but at the same time, they kind of roll their eyes. To Manhattan's Central Park, grown from hundreds of rocky acres. Central Park is really America's first urban park. Next, City Parks on Modern Marvels. What could be an oil painting by the father of Impressionism, Claude Monet, is but a blink away from joggers training for a marathon. Around each corner, hikers discover something new to delight the eye. And skaters ballet on ice in the shadow of skyscrapers. Today's city park is a living organism embraced Today's city park is a living organism, embracing both Victorian sensibilities and the desires of this century's active population. It's a magical place whose visitors, rich and poor, share in a democratic dream first realized over 150 years ago. Born of pure necessity, the urban park was the only escape from the clutter, clamor, and filth of the industrialized America's earliest city parks faced nearly insurmountable obstacles, including difficult natural terrain, as well as lack of modern machinery. San Francisco's Golden Gate Park grew out of the sand. Los Angeles's Griffith Park was the brainchild of a madman. And out of the middle of Manhattan, once a rocky wasteland, grew an oasis known as Central Park.
