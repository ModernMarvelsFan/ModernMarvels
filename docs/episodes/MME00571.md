> **Opening Hook:**

<summary><b>Click to view full opening narration (~2 minutes)</b></summary>

> *"They're pushy. They're screwy. Real suckers. But flip a switch, and they transform deserts into destinations and empower motorists to fill her up. Strong enough to cut through steel or gentle enough to milk a cow. These machines don't go with the flow. They generate it. Now, the hidden world of pumps on Modern Marvels. The pump, in all its various incarnations, is a relatively simple machine. After all, it performs just one basic function, transferring liquids or viscous materials from point A to point B. Perhaps it's this single purpose that masks its importance in our everyday lives. For the pump is certainly one of mankind's most indispensable forms of technology. Pumps are everywhere because there's liquid everywhere. We contact something that has to do with liquid almost everything we do. The most precious liquid that pumps convey is the very essence of life itself, water. This is Lake Matthews, located near Los Angeles, California. The lake holds 59 billion gallons of water. That's enough to meet the needs of roughly 1.5 million people for an entire year. But the lake isn't connected to any other natural body of water, and the annual rainfall here is a paltry 11 inches. So how does the water get here? This water came all the way from the Colorado River"*
