<summary><b>Click to view full opening narration (~2 minutes)</b></summary>

> *"We eat, and to get it, we bake, boil, and blast. It's essential to life, but too much of it could kill you. It's made from two volatile elements. But it's a miracle mineral, ideal for everything from clearing roads. If we don't do the job right, someone gets hurt. To cleaning your pool. It's even perfect for burning rubber. Once you go 200 out here, then you want to go a little faster. Now, salt on Modern Marbles. This unassuming mineral essential to our survival is a mover and shaker in a class by a man. itself. It's salt, and throughout history, those controlling it have been the masters of their domain. Two of today's superpowers, China and the United States, rank one and two in salt production, and each nation's salt heritage runs deep. China's emperors financed construction of the Great Wall with profits from their lucrative salt trade. And Salt was the principal cargo on the Erie Canal as a young America pushed west. Part of salt's value lies in its versatility. It has 14,000 known uses, from softening water to killing poison ivy. We all know it adds flavor to our food. But did you know that it's a vital ingredient in glass, rubber, paint, and bug spray? Salt is the name that chemists give to a metal and nonmetal compound. The most commonly known salt is sodium chloride, or NACL, which features sodium metal and chlorine gas. Incredibly, the two elements that comprise the stable, ingestible salt we all know and love are two very reactive and sometimes dangerous substances."*
