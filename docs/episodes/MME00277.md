Its name was created from the words acceleration and excellence, and many believe the Acela, with its state-of-the-art design, power, and speed, could revolutionize the way we travel. The Acela was the truly first new train developed in America. developed in America in 30 years. So we're basically breaking ground. Launched in the winter of 2000 at a cost of $800 million, the Acela rockets between Boston and Washington, DC, at speeds reaching 150 miles an hour. The Acela takes about 2 and 1⁄2 hours to travel between New and Washington, and three hours and 18 minutes from New York to Boston. The New York Boston leg is now about 90 minutes faster than earlier trains. It has power to spare. It's kind of the sports car of the industry right now. During the Acela's journey north of New York, there are so many twists and turns that if you were to fit them together, they would create 10 full circles. The Acela can take the turns at speeds higher than conventional trains because of its unique tilting cars. As the Acela enters a curve, a computer in the lead car adjusts hydraulic jacks in the train's wheel sets. As a result, the passenger cars will tilt by as much as four degrees depending on the speed of the train and sharpness of the curve. We have a series of transponders that are embedded in the track structure that actually give location to the train so the train knows what it's supposed to be doing at various locations.
