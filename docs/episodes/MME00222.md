### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Next, wheels rolled across the oceans as vital components of ships And long-distance trade made the importance of accurate timetables A new challenge that the promise of wheels would solve Although humans began settling in villages approximately 10,000 years ago It would be another 5,000 years before archaeological evidence of wheels would appear The Wheel will return on Modern Marvels During the late Middle Ages, as trade increased partly due to the application of wheels Merchants looked across the seas to new markets for buying and selling goods Large ships were built, using steering wheels to maneuver the rudder and direct the ship As the boat gets bigger, a tiller doesn't give you enough advantage, mechanical advantage, on the rudder."*

<summary><b>Click to view full opening narration (~2 minutes)</b></summary>

> *"The invention of the wheel, if I may put it this way, invented a mind. And in becoming inventors, we begin to think of ourselves differently. Not simply imperiled by the facts of the world, but perhaps competing with the facts of the world, as in a contest. And a contest that we plausibly now might regard ourselves as winning. The simple, elegant, yet revolutionary machine called the wheel has literally transformed mankind. Not just in the way we navigate the challenges of our lives, but in the very way we perceive ourselves. ourselves. Quite a responsibility for a tool so ubiquitous that we encounter hundreds in a day without even realizing it. So simple that we struggle to adequately define it. I think a wheel is any circular object, regardless of its width, that rolls. And that means it might have an axle to transmit its power to someplace else or to direct its power at a certain point. Some say an axle is not a necessary component of a wheel. You can imagine wheels in which the mechanical wheel is not linked to that central axis, for example, in a ball bearing. A ball bearing goes around and makes a circular motion. It fulfills our definition of a wheel. But it doesn't have an axle. The wheel and axle is one of six basic machines. Human-made objects that are the basis of all devices that provide us with mechanical advantage over our environment. The other five include the lever, the inclined plane, the pulley, the wedge, and the screw. You know, it's interesting how some of these machines, it becomes a vague distinction between them."*
