> *"It's at the core of our planet's $7 trillion construction industry, sheltering more than half of the world's population. Basically, it's the primary building material. It teems with billions of living organisms and can move on its own with deadly results. The Dust Bowl in the 1930s was the greatest environmental disaster in American history. So whether you're playing in the mud or racing on it, you'll never get your hands dirty the same way again on Modern Marvel's Down and Dirty. You know the phrase, dull as dirt? Well, it's not. We think of it just as a common thing. It's, I don't want my kids tracking it into the house. But if we didn't have dirt, we wouldn't have life on Earth. Just a little of it can support a universe. A clump of soil this size has as many organisms in it as all the people on the Earth. But a lot of people on Earth live in dirt, too. It's used today to house about half the people that live on the planet Earth. It's also purifier. Water drains through clean, good soil and comes out pure, clean. We can drink it. We spend a lot of time digging in dirt because it holds a wealth of valuable materials, and in it hide the secrets of ancient civilizations. Most of us are surrounded by dirt all of our lives and add to it when we die. To keep us on the move, we move an awful lot of dirt while building millions of miles of roads, highways, and freeways. Usually we top off transportation infrastructure with a layer of concrete or asphalt."*
