### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, Yankee ingenuity in the home with household wonders on Modern Marvels."*

> They save us time. They save us money. They've changed our lives in 100 ways. Household appliances. The vacuum was invented because of a sneeze. The sewing machine caused riots. And the washing machine had over 1,000 patents before it was motorized. Now, Yankee ingenuity in the home with household wonders on Modern Marvels. This lady's calling her husband. This lady's calling her mother. And this lady, she's calling her oven. Sound funny? Actually, it's something that could happen in the future. The fulfillment of the American dream, a kitchen that runs itself, an oven that bakes by remote control. That was the future in the 1950s, a wall to wall electrical wonder filled with the newest blender, mixer, defroster, and disposal, all turned on with the touch of a button or the turn of a dial. But the miracle of this household technology was hard earned. Each of these inventions tells the story of the daily life of a nation and even the world. And their story begins with the revolution. It was America's second revolution, but it was heralded not by the fire of muskets or roar of a cannon, but by the whir of wheels and cogs and the clatter of the factory. It was the industrial revolution, the age of manufacturing, mass production, automation, and invention. It began in the middle of the 19th century, and it forever changed our lives. A new middle class was being born. Domestic servants, once a staple in the average home, were leaving for a better job in the factories. They were becoming independent, and you couldn't blame them.
