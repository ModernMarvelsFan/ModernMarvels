### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, Traps on Modern Marvels."*

> When you least expect it, these mechanical beasts bite down and never let go. A death sentence for rodents. He's not going anywhere. A beacon of hope for fish. A tool for cleaning up New Orleans. Or a human intruder's worst nightmare. Don't touch anything and you won't be harmed. Simple in concept, yet advanced in their engineering. They pushed the American frontier to the West and created a division in its people. You'd better watch your step. Now, Traps on Modern Marvels. We now return to Traps on Modern Marvels. Randolph County, West Virginia. It's part of the 920,000 acres of Monongahela National Forest. It's home to nearly 4,000 American black bears. And every year, experts from the West Virginia Division of Natural Resources Black Bear Management Program are out to trap them as part of an ongoing statewide population study. The beastly challenge of trapping an animal that can weigh up to 400 pounds is handled with snares, a type of modified lasso. They must be powerful enough to capture and restrain the bears, yet incorporate a humane design that won't injure them. The first step in building the snare is to pick a tree. When we're looking for the snare spot, we're looking for just that perfect tree, not one that's too big that we can't get the cable around. not one too small that he's going to break it off the next morning. We're looking for a nice medium-sized tree, like this maple right here. Chris Ryan will use the tree as an anchor for the snare. He chains a wire loop to the trunk. This is a spring we put on the snares just for safety, just like on a porch swing, so that it's not just hooked to the tree real solid, so that the actual snare will give.
