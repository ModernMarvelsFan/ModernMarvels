Randolph County, West Virginia. It's part of the 920,000 acres of Monongahela National Forest. It's home to nearly 4,000 American black bears. And every year, experts from the West Virginia Division of Natural Resources Black Bear Management Program are out to trap them as part of an ongoing statewide population study. The beastly challenge of trapping an animal that can weigh up to 400 pounds is handled with snares, a type of modified lasso. They must be powerful enough to capture and restrain the bears, yet incorporate a humane design that won't injure them. The first step in building the snare is to pick a tree. When we're looking for the snare spot, we're looking for just that perfect tree, not one that's too big that we can't get the cable around. not one too small that he's going to break it off the next morning. We're looking for a nice medium-sized tree, like this maple right here. Chris Ryan will use the tree as an anchor for the snare. He chains a wire loop to the trunk.
