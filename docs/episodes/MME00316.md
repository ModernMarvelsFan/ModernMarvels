### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, Bullets on Modern Marvels."*

> They can rip through armor and light up the sky, travel at speeds up to 3,000 miles an hour. They can be designed to kill or just disable. Some of them seem to think for themselves. Now, Bullets on Modern Marvels. After 9-11, Americans realized they were vulnerable. Security was dramatically tightened, particularly at airports and on planes. Air marshals armed with handguns were assigned to flights, and some pilots began carrying pistols in the cockpit. Suddenly, a blazing shootout with hijackers at 30,000 feet in a pressurized passenger cabin was a real possibility. There's a popular belief that a single bullet hole through an airplane's fuselage will cause a depressurization so violent that the plane will break up in midair. That is simply not true. What does concern aviation safety experts are stray bullets hitting passengers. There is a special kind of bullet that substantially reduces the chance of a ricocheting shot hitting an innocent passenger. It's called the frangible. Unlike conventional bullets that remain intact, frangibles penetrate flesh but break up into small, less harmful pieces upon contact with anything hard. Conventional projectiles are made with a lead core and a copper jacket. jacket. Now we have centering and copper matrix technology and other metals that don't have to use a jacket. And so basically the bullet turns to dust when it hits a steel target or any really hard object. This has gone a long way to reducing the overpenetration problem.
