> *"When the sun goes down, it's a whole different world out there. Mysterious, thrilling, and sometimes very scary. Light is the key to rounding up thousands of pounds of writhing nocturnal creatures. Technology helps the military detect the enemy. Cops catch culprits. And jet pilots see what's ahead when there's blackness beyond the cockpit. And a dark sky provides the perfect backdrop for dazzling spectacles. Now, the secrets of night on Modern Marvels. Somewhere in the dark on the surface of the Pacific Ocean, fishermen are seeking an elusive prize. a prize that can be best won at night. Squid in the hundreds of thousands. The particular species of squid they're after are scientifically known as loligo opalescens, commonly called market squid. They're found along the west coast of North America, from the Gulf of Alaska to Baja California. But the fishery is concentrated in Southern California, where the squid spawn. For the crew of the Barbara H, this particular day begins about 50 miles north of Los Angeles, at a time when most fishermen are returning with a morning's catch. It's one of dozens of squid boats setting out at the same time, hoping to beat the others in a high-stakes competition waged on the open ocean, while most of America is asleep. Squid season is during their spawning season, when these nocturnal sea creatures swim closer to the surface and nearer to the coastline. But their behavior is unpredictable, and they are often impossible to find. After five hours of heading west, the Barbara H is getting close to where squid have appeared recently. We're about 30 miles off the coast of Ventura at the Channel"*
