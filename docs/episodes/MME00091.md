### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, Battlefield Medicine on Modern Marvels."*

<details>
<summary><b>Click to view full opening narration (~2 minutes)</b></summary>

> *"The violence and slaughter of war. The urgency and hope of medicine. From the field surgeries of the ancient world, to the amputation tables of the Civil War, to the medevac dramas of Vietnam, an army within all armies has fought a war within all wars, trying to revive the wounded and save lives. Now, Battlefield Medicine on Modern Marvels. He who would become a surgeon should join the army and follow it. So Hippocrates, father of medicine, counseled nearly 2,500 years ago. If war has a silver lining, perhaps this is it. It's the ultimate hands-on classroom in raw anatomy and mutilated flesh. A horrific classroom that teaches above all what the human species has been willing to do to itself. There is something paradoxical about the very idea of battlefield medicine, about armies and individuals desperately trying to kill and rescue at the same time. Louis Pasteur, the famous scientist, said that there are two forces in this world that are constantly at play. One is a force of always designing new weapons of destruction and some of them of mass destruction. And the other force is always trying to find new cures for diseases and how to save lives. And he said, which one of these forces will win out at the end, God alone knows. It may seem like mass destruction has gotten the upper hand. Modern weapons make the very notion of battlefield medicine seem obsolete. But the weapons and strategies of rescue have also evolved, benefiting at times from the same technology that propels the slaughter. The history of battlefield medicine is almost as old as the history of organized war."*

</details>
