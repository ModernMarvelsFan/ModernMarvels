### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, Hadrian's Wall on Modern Marvels."*

> A 2,000-year-old stone wall bisects rural England. Was it once a fortress and a barrier, built by ancient invaders to mark the furthest reaches of their empire? Who was it designed to exclude? And who did it protect? Now, Hadrian's Wall on Modern Marvels. There was a seam that cuts through the patchwork fields and pastures of northern England. A rock and mortar scar, 80 Roman miles long and 2,000 years old. Hadrian's Wall persists as a relic of an ancient Roman past, chained to the boggy soil and linking our modern time to its ancient one. Mute, cold, weathered. Observer to an endless parade of kings and thieves, farmers and soldiers. Hadrian's Wall keeps many secrets. For something so solid, tangible and real, its early history peers tentatively from the vague corners of its past. Roman history has left us with virtually no information about this northern frontier. I think there are two sentences in the whole of Roman history which actually refer to Hadrian's Wall, for example. According to one ancient historian, Hadrian was the first to build a wall 80 miles long to separate the Romans and the barbarians. They're right, it was 80 miles long, but he didn't build it to separate the Romans from the barbarians. I mean, there's just as many barbarians to the south of the wall as there are to the north.
