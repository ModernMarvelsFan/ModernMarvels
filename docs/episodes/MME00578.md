### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Dams will return on Modern Marvels."*

> Millions of tons of concrete hold back the force of a river. Generators spin out billions of kilowatt hours to power cities. Potable water flows for hundreds of miles. Irrigation provides food for entire nations. The world's largest structures restrain the fury of nature's floods of what happens when disaster strikes. It's engineering at its most massive scale with both benefit and cost to the environment. Now, Dams on Modern Marbles. Water. It's a giver and taker of life. With it, barren land can be cultivated. Without it, no life can be sustained. Because human history is replete with cycles of drought and flood, we've sought both to conserve and to protect ourselves from this destructive yet essential resource. If you're going to develop the resources of a nation, you want to not let the river just flow into the sea without making some use of it. And we maximize our water use with dams. There are an estimated 900,000 dams worldwide. Most are small to medium sized. But 40,000 are over 50 feet high. The most familiar dams, the mega dams, A select club of about 300 over 450 feet high are vital pieces of civilization's infrastructure. In the 1930s, we in the U.S. realized that we could manage entire river basins, not just one single dam at a time, but dozens. So we began developing river basins on a vast scale.
