### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, Deliver It on Modern Marvels."*

> Need something delivered? Call on these transport pros. Some move luxury yachts and lengthy wind turbine blades. Others get prized horses to the track. You can even get dinner and a movie brought to your door. We'll ship well over 2 million DVDs in any given day. To save precious time, bike messengers battle New York City gridlock. At the speed that I'm traveling, if I hit somebody on this bike, somebody's gonna get seriously hurt. But this delicate delivery just might save your life. Now, Deliver It on Modern Marvels. Almost all the goods necessary to our lives deliver by ship, by train, by truck, and by plane. Each year, the U.S. Postal Service alone delivers one billion Priority and Express packages and documents, as well as additional billions of pieces of first-class mail. Count on FedEx for another two billion packages and documents. And UPS, the world's largest package delivery company, is good for four billion. But to deliver a certain supersized item that weighs over six tons and is about one-third the length of a football field requires other shipping expertise. These are wind turbine blades at the port of Houston. With the need and desire for renewable energy on the rise, wind turbine systems have seen a spike in production. Companies like GE Energy are making and shipping systems as quickly as possible. The wind industry in the U.S. has grown dramatically over the last few years, and GE as the number one supplier has a big part of that. So last year we shipped over 3,000 wind turbines. Another way to think about the number of wind turbines is about one every three hours. Every three hours another wind turbine comes off the factory line and has to go through this whole logistic process to the project site. Our customers are buying from us about a year to two years in advance. Built in Brazil, these blades make the non-stop trip to Houston in about three weeks. GE has designed these special fixtures enabling them to be carried two at a time.
