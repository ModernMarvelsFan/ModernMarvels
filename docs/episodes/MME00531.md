### 🎙️ Show Introduction

> **Opening Hook:**  
> *"Now, world's biggest machines on Modern Marvels."*

> A wind tunnel with 40-foot diameter fans. A flight simulator that's eight stories high. A joyride that makes Big Ben look small. A machine that pulls super film 10 miles long on a 200-pound reel. A mower that can cut your lawn in seconds. And a 300,000-pound rock eater. Now, world's biggest machines on Modern Marvels. For the last 100 years, machines have flown us around the globe and propelled us into space. Today, engineers and scientists are creating new machines to take us farther, faster than ever before. And those machines need to be tested. The 80 by 120 subsonic wind tunnel, biggest in the world. Test section, 80 feet wide, 120 feet high. Airflow drive system, six 40-foot diameter fans. NASA Ames Research Center, Mountain View, California. Current manager, United States Air Force. There's a lot we don't understand about how aircraft operate. We try to push the edge of the envelope with every new development to get a little bit more performance or a little bit safer performance out of aircraft. Wind tunnels are a very big part of making that job happen, of making both air travel safe for us and for making aircraft of the future having even better performance than what we have today. Usually, wind tunnels employ scale models of aircraft models of aircraft for testing.
