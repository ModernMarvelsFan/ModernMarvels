They are the crucial link in an ever-increasing number of tasks, such as weather forecasting, global telephone, fax and television communication, military and commercial reconnaissance, and even navigation. But just over 50 years ago, satellites were simply an idea in the mind of a British scientist famous for his science fiction writing. Arthur C. Clarke was the first man to envision many of the modern applications of satellites. He also penned 2001 A Space Odyssey. And in 1945, 12 years before Sputnik's launch, he wrote an article in Wireless World magazine describing how satellites could be placed in geostationary orbit and used to relay signals. Today, most communication satellites do operate in this orbit, rotating at the same rate as the Earth because of their location, 22,300 miles above the equator. In addition, Clark foresaw polar orbiting and weather satellites"*
